Hosting, domains, and DNS are often lumped together—but they’re three **separate pieces** of your website’s foundation. Understanding the difference helps you avoid outages, surprise bills, and migration disasters.

Here’s the plain-English breakdown.

---

## What Is a Domain?

Your **domain** is your website’s address.

Example: bsquaredsolutions.io

It’s what people type into a browser to find you.

### Key things to know:
- Domains are purchased from registrars (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Google Domains)
- You typically renew them yearly
- You can move a domain between providers

**You should always own your domain outright.**

---

## What Is Hosting?

**Hosting** is where your website actually lives.

It’s the server that stores:
- Your files and images
- Your database
- Your code or CMS

### Common hosting types:
- **Shared hosting** – cheap, limited performance
- **Managed WordPress hosting** – optimized, easier maintenance
- **Static hosting** – fast and secure (Netlify, Vercel)
- **Cloud / VPS hosting** – scalable, more control

Hosting directly affects **speed, security, and reliability**.

---

## What Is DNS?

DNS (Domain Name System) is the internet’s address book.

It connects your **domain name** to your **hosting server**.

In simple terms:
- Your domain says *who you are*
- Your hosting says *where your site lives*
- DNS says *how to get there*

Without DNS, your domain doesn’t know where to send visitors.

---

## How They Work Together

1. You buy a domain
2. You choose a hosting provider
3. DNS records point the domain to the host
4. Visitors type your domain
5. Your website loads

If any one of these breaks, your site goes down.

---

## Common Small Business Mistakes
- Letting a developer register the domain in their name
- Not knowing where DNS is managed
- Choosing hosting based only on price
- No backups or SSL certificates

---

## Best Practices
- Register domains in your own account
- Use reliable DNS providers (Cloudflare, Google)
- Choose hosting based on performance and support
- Keep a shared document with all credentials

---

## Final Thought

Hosting, domains, and DNS aren’t complicated—they’re just invisible until something breaks. A little understanding goes a long way in protecting your site and your business.

If your site feels slow, unstable, or hard to manage, the issue often starts here.
