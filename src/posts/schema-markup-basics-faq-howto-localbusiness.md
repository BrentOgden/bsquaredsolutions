Schema markup is one of the most misunderstood—and underused—SEO tools for small businesses. It doesn’t change how your site *looks*, but it dramatically improves how search engines **understand** your content.

This guide explains schema markup in plain English and shows where it actually matters.

---

## What Is Schema Markup?

Schema markup is **structured data** added to your website’s code that tells search engines what your content *means*, not just what it says.

Instead of guessing, Google gets clarity.

Examples:
- “This is a business”
- “These are FAQs”
- “This is a step-by-step process”

Schema helps your site qualify for **rich results** in search.

---

## Why Schema Markup Matters

Schema markup can:
- Improve click-through rates
- Enable rich search features
- Reduce ambiguity for search engines
- Support local SEO visibility

It doesn’t guarantee rankings—but it *does* increase how your listings appear.

---

## FAQ Schema

**FAQ schema** tells Google that a page contains real questions and answers.

### Best use cases:
- Service pages
- Pricing pages
- Support or help sections

### Benefits:
- Expands search results with dropdown answers
- Takes up more screen space
- Pre-qualifies visitors before they click

Important rule:  
Only mark up FAQs that are **visible on the page**.

---

## HowTo Schema

**HowTo schema** defines step-by-step instructions.

### Best use cases:
- Tutorials
- Setup guides
- Processes or workflows

### Benefits:
- Eligible for rich step displays
- Clear structure for complex topics
- Better mobile presentation

HowTo schema works best when steps are concise and clearly ordered.

---

## LocalBusiness Schema

**LocalBusiness schema** is essential for service-based businesses.

### It defines:
- Business name
- Address
- Phone number
- Hours
- Service area

### Why it matters:
- Reinforces Google Business Profile data
- Improves local search confidence
- Reduces NAP (Name, Address, Phone) inconsistencies

This schema should exist on your homepage or contact page.

---

## JSON-LD: The Preferred Format

Most schema today is added using **JSON-LD**, which:
- Lives in the page `<head>`
- Doesn’t affect design
- Is easy to maintain

Google officially recommends JSON-LD over inline markup.

---

## Common Schema Mistakes
- Marking up content that isn’t visible
- Using incorrect schema types
- Copy-pasting schema without customization
- Forgetting to update schema when content changes

Bad schema is worse than no schema.

---

## How Much Schema Do You Really Need?

Start small:
1. LocalBusiness schema
2. FAQ schema on key pages
3. HowTo schema where applicable

You don’t need schema everywhere—just where it adds clarity.

---

## Final Thought

Schema markup helps search engines trust what your site is about. It’s not flashy, but it’s one of the cleanest ways to improve how your business appears in search results.

If your SEO foundation is solid, schema is how you add polish.
